# Storytelling & Script — Product Videos That Get Shared

Research-backed rules for writing the video before animating anything. The script and beat sheet come first; motion serves story.

## Hooks (0–3s)

1. **The first frame IS the hook.** No logo intro, no music ramp-up alone. First on-screen line lands within ~0.5s; the swipe decision happens in 0.5–1.5s and ~70% of viewers are gone by second four if not engaged.
2. **Stack three hooks simultaneously**: visual (motion/scale/unexpected image), text (bold claim), and audio (first VO sentence or sound). All fire in second one.
3. **Proven hook archetypes**: contrarian belief, negative warning, hyper-specific outcome ("It replied to 32 emails while I was asleep"), us-vs-them, visual interrupt, mid-action open, curiosity gap.
4. **A static product shot never stops the scroll.** Open on action or transformation already in motion.
5. **Write 10–20 hook candidates, keep the sharpest.** Optimize the hook for reaction (curiosity, tension), not full comprehension. Tension, not ragebait.
6. **Specific numbers beat adjectives** — "Replied to 32 emails overnight" > "blazingly productive."

## Structure selection

- **Problem → Agitate → Reveal (PAS)** — default for launches; audience feels pain, doesn't know solutions exist.
- **Demo-as-story (Linear-style)** — audience already cares about your winning axis; prove it with relentless real-UI velocity, no context-setting.
- **Day-in-the-life / while-you-slept** — for agents/assistants where value is accumulated background work.
- **Before/After** — one dramatic transformation.
- **"What if" speculative** — category creation; must be paired with real demo or reads as vapor.

Rules:
- Sell the outcome, not the demo. Problem-led storytelling lifts conversion up to ~80% over feature-first.
- Agitate with a concrete scene, not an abstraction: "Every Monday, someone pulls numbers from five tabs into a spreadsheet" — not "teams struggle with fragmentation."
- The reveal must land by **15–20s**.
- Show **one workflow exceptionally well** — never a feature tour.
- Borrow credibility with recognizable logos in frame (Gmail, Slack, Notion connected to the product).

## Beat sheet: "While You Were Sleeping" (45s) — the agent-assistant template

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Hook | "While you were asleep, this happened." + evidence already piling up |
| 3–7s | Setup | Human done for the day — ONE shot only (phone face-down, lights off, clock) |
| 7–25s | Agent montage | Timestamped actions (2:14 AM — replied to Stripe; 3:37 AM — drafted the investor update). Real UI, chat threads, checkmarks. 5–7 cuts on beat. Timestamps are the specificity that sells it. |
| 25–33s | Morning reveal | Sunrise + ONE summary card ("Overnight: 32 emails triaged, 3 meetings scheduled, brief drafted"). Music drop here. |
| 33–39s | Human approves | The believability beat — user scans, taps approve. Human stays the hero. |
| 39–45s | Close | Brand + one-liner + single CTA |

Other templates: PAS 60s (hook 0–3, problem 3–10, reveal 10–15 on the drop, one-workflow capabilities 15–45, proof 45–52, close 52–60); demo-as-story 30s for X (mid-action open 0–2, near-uncut demo 2–20, payoff number 20–26, close 26–30).

## Hook and prop discipline (defect classes that recur)

- **Frame 0 is the thumbnail.** No black frames, no logo warmups, no static shot held >0.5s before the first event. The hook element is on screen at frame 0, its first state change lands inside 15 frames, and the claim line is animating by frame 30.
- **Genre tropes need earning.** "Dark room + clock + AI worked overnight" is the most common open in the agent genre. If you use it, subvert or compress it — or open on the most surprising receipt instead ("Your investor update went out at 5:02. You were asleep.") and rewind.
- **Props are testimony — they must not contradict the trust model.** Every status string, timestamp, and chip is evidence in the viewer's cross-examination. If the trust story is "nothing ships without you," nothing may appear pre-queued or auto-sent.
- **The human acts on camera.** At the decision moment, show the gesture (cursor glides in, taps Approve, then vanishes as automation takes over). An approval that approves itself empties the film's own safety claim.
- **The close line is the earned thesis.** End on the sentence the film just proved, not the brand's generic tagline — if they differ, the film wins. Every statement headline and the close must read as one voice.
- **Verb series need semantic range.** Drafted/Written = the same act twice; Drafted/Prepped/Cleared/Planned = four different powers.

## Believable vs cringe for AI-agent narratives

- **Believable = real UI receipts.** Timestamps, real tool names, plausible message text, notification stacks, a morning summary card.
- **Cringe = AI replacing human meaning.** Never show the agent doing what audiences believe a human should do (heartfelt writing, relationships, creativity — the Gemini "Dear Sydney" failure). Show it doing what humans resent: scheduling, triage, follow-ups, status reports, drafts-for-review.
- **Never show AI authoring the user's own public voice.** "It wrote this post while I slept" is a quote-tweet dunk magnet — it confesses to the exact slop accusation the audience already holds. The agent PREPARES from the human's real work (changelog, commits, notes); the human authors and approves. Frame it as "drafted from what you shipped — your voice, your call."
- **The human stays hero and approver.** Always include a review/approve beat. The agent drafts; the human decides.
- Time-compression: one ~2s shot (clock / dark window / phone face-down) establishes "asleep." Never spend more than one scene on the human sleeping.

## Length & pacing

- X/Twitter: 25–45s (sweet spot 25–35s). Landing hero: 30–60s. Shorts: 15–30s.
- Assume **sound-off** on feeds: on-screen text alone must carry the whole story.
- A 45s video ≈ **12–18 scenes**. Fastest cutting 0–10s (~5 scenes in the first 10s), then 2–3s average, with exactly two deliberate holds: the reveal and the end card.
- Videos under 60s hold ~71% of viewers. When in doubt, cut.

## On-screen copy

- One idea per line, one line at a time. Selling two ideas sells zero.
- **Max ~6 words per line.** Longer → split into two beats.
- **Verb-first lines**: "Drafts your replies." "Books the flight." "Clears your inbox."
- Apple one-liner toolkit: economy of words, "you/your" address, contrast pairs ("Small chip. Giant leap."), single-word sentences ("Done."), quotable soundbites.
- One or two fonts total.

## Music & emotional arc

- Define the emotional register before the shot list — it sets BPM, cut pace, visual style.
- Cuts land on the beat; **the reveal lands on the drop**. If the reveal misses a musically satisfying moment, the whole video feels off.
- Standard 45–60s music map: sparse/tense under the problem → riser into the reveal → drop at reveal → groove under capabilities → strip back to a clean resolve under the end card (music "exhales" so the brand line breathes).

## Close & CTA

- **One CTA, ever.** Specific and outcome-linked ("Start free — no credit card") beats "Learn more."
- End card: mark + one-liner + CTA, high contrast, **held 3–4s**, stylistically continuous with the video. Strongest proof point placed near the end, where the trust decision happens.
- On feed platforms, echo the product name/URL mid-video — many viewers never reach the end.

## The director's interview (ask before writing a single scene)

Ask the user these, in order, before scripting. High-impact only — skip any already answered by the brief:

1. Who is the ONE viewer this is for, and what do they already believe? (picks the structure)
2. If they remember exactly one sentence tomorrow, what is it? (becomes the one-liner + end card)
3. What is the single "holy-shit" proof moment — the real thing on screen that makes the claim undeniable? (becomes the drop moment)
4. What does your user's worst 20 minutes look like without the product — the actual scene, not the category problem? (fuels the agitation beat)
5. What should it feel like — and 1–2 reference videos for the vibe? (register → music, pacing, art direction)
6. Where does it live first (X / landing hero / YouTube) and is the goal shares, signups, or investor heat? (length, sound-off design, CTA)
7. What is the single CTA — and what must we never claim or show? (honesty boundary; keeps agent videos believable)
