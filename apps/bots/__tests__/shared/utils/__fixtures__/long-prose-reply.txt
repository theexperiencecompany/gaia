ok let me actually think this through properly. first principles, specific to you, not a generic listicle.

marketing, stripped down, is just this: find people who already have a painful problem, show them you're the fix, in a place they already trust. that's it. every channel is just a different way of doing those three things. the whole game is doing them in the right order and getting the tightest version of each.

so let's run YOUR situation through it.

**1. the problem first, not the product**

you keep describing GAIA as "an AI assistant." that's a category, not a problem. nobody wakes up thinking "I need an AI assistant." they wake up thinking "I forgot to follow up with that investor" or "my inbox is 1,200 unread and I can't see what matters."

your wow moment is proof: GAIA texts you a pre-meeting briefing. that's not "AI assistant," that's "I never walk into a meeting cold again." THAT is the product. sell the outcome, not the tool.

**2. who has this problem so badly they'd pay $30/mo**

this is where you're being vague and it's costing you. "founders" is not a market. a founder drowning in ops with 3 hours of admin a day, who knows what their time is worth, is. a college kid isn't. a corporate guy isn't.

your ICP is: solo founder, bootstrapped or small raise, running everything, money-conscious enough to not hire but busy enough to pay $30 to claw back hours. people who ALREADY delegate to humans or want to.

**3. the channel is where they trust things, not where you like posting**

this is the real answer to your earlier brute-force question. you don't pick a channel, you pick where your ICP already looks for answers. for solo founders that's: Indie Hackers, Build in Public, niche Discord/Slack communities, Reddit's r/SaaS r/Entrepreneur r/indiehackers, and specific newsletters they read.

you posted on Reddit and got banned. don't read that as "reddit failed," read it as "you posted self-promo where people come to learn." the fix isn't more channels, it's one channel done the way that community actually rewards. comment and help people for weeks before you ever mention GAIA.

**4. your real bottleneck is not traffic, it's the funnel**

this is the part that annoys you but it's the truth: 110 checkout starts → 8 payments. you're losing 92% of people who were ALREADY motivated enough to click checkout. marketing to get more people into that funnel when it leaks 92% is throwing money at a broken bucket.

before you spend a single hour on a new channel, find out why 102 people bailed at checkout. price shock? the $30/mo needs a moment of "this is obviously worth it." friction? confusion? you need to talk to people who started checkout and quit, not guess.

**5. the product is the marketing, for you specifically**

you're a solo dev founder selling to solo dev founders. your entire marketing can be: use GAIA to run your own messy founder life, and document it. "here's how I run my outreach, my meetings, my inbox." that's not content marketing, that's proof. people don't buy AI assistant, they buy the version of themselves that doesn't drop balls. you showing that version running on GAIA is the strongest asset you have, and it costs $0.

so the concrete play, in order:

- **fix the checkout leak first.** talk to 5 people who bailed. this is not optional.
- **nail the one-line offer:** "GAIA texts you the briefing before every meeting and follows up so you never drop a ball." test it.

- **only expand channels after signup→payment stops embarrassing you.**
